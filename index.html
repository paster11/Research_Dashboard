BlackRock Investment Institute

37 weeklies, Jan 5 – Oct 5, 2026 · Latest: "U.S. dollar: surprisingly resilient" Oct 5 edition — no positioning change vs Sep 28

Key Insights

  • The dollar is up ~4% this year against DM currencies and has retraced most of its post-April-2 tariff decline — "too soon to write off the dollar's long-term role," even as BII sees it as stronger than fundamentals imply.
  • Markets now price roughly four more Fed hikes over 12 months after last month's increase; BII pushes back: "we think that is too much."
  • Foreign investors put nearly $1 trillion into U.S. equities and funds in the 12 months to July, including a record $426bn in Q2 — earnings growth should keep these inflows coming.
  • The 10-year UST yield climbed to 5.34%, its highest since 2002; a soft U.S. jobs report capped further gains. The September FOMC hike was the Fed's first in three years.
  • Fiscal backdrop stays challenging ($1.9tn deficit, public debt ~101% of GDP) — not a current dollar driver, but flagged as a risk that "could return to the fore."

Key Takeaways (positioning)

  • No new OW/N/UW flips this edition — every asset-class call unchanged vs Sep 28, including EM equities OW and Europe short/medium govt bonds N.
  • U.S. and EM equities and AI overweights maintained (AI bottlenecks: power, chips, data centers); prefer short/medium govt bonds over long duration; selective in credit favoring clear cash flows and higher-rated HY.
  • Dollar view is qualitative, not a tactical OW/N/UW line: expect it to stay "around current levels, if not somewhat weaker" — limited scope for a sustained dollar bull run; a weaker dollar would further support the EM equity overweight.
  • Alpha ideas: macro hedge funds, venture capital, market-neutral strategies, selected private credit, non-U.S. alpha.

Key Outlook

  • Structural forces behind long-term yields stay intact even as near-term Fed-expectation shifts move rates; earnings growth should keep foreign inflows into U.S. equities going.
  • Fiscal risk (deficit, debt load) not a present dollar driver but a latent one — could resurface as a theme.
  • Slower U.S. labor-supply growth means softer payroll gains need not signal a weaker economy — consistent with BII's recent framing.

Key Monitoring

  • Oct 7: FOMC minutes from the September meeting — gauge how hawkish the discussion was.
  • Oct 9: University of Michigan preliminary consumer sentiment, after the late-September reading was its second-lowest ever.
  • Oct 6: U.S. international trade data; EU/UK construction PMI.
  • Fed-hike repricing (market now pricing ~4 more hikes in 12 months) vs. BII's view that this is overstated.
