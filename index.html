BlackRock Investment Institute

35 weeklies, Jan 5 – Sep 21, 2026 · Latest: "The AI buildout meets the 5% world" Sep 21 edition — no positioning change vs Sep 14

Key Insights

  • Accelerating AI buildout is intensifying competition for capital against heavy government borrowing; estimated annual U.S. financing demand could reach ~$7.5–8tn by 2030.
  • AI/data-center bond issuance is ~14% of U.S. investment-grade issuance this year, up from 5% in 2025 and ~1% over the prior decade.
  • The Fed's first rate hike in three years helped reestablish policy credibility; the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5%.
  • Middle East conflict still a live pressure: Brent crude back above $100/bbl, adding to sticky, supply-driven inflation.
  • Most AI compute demand currently comes from inference rather than training, so demand should stay robust even if frontier-model progress slows.

Key Takeaways (positioning)

  • No new OW/N/UW flips this edition — all calls unchanged vs Sep 14, including the prior week's EM equities OW and Europe short/medium govt bonds N.
  • U.S. equities and AI overweights maintained; favor short/medium-term govt bonds over long duration.
  • In credit, prefer attractive coupons away from the weakest borrowers and the largest debt issuers.
  • Broader stance: pro-risk but not complacent; ready to reassess if AI earnings disappoint or higher long-term rates raise the equity return hurdle further.

Key Outlook

  • A yield rise driven by resilient growth and central-bank credibility can coexist with the pro-risk stance; a rise driven by inflation/credibility concerns would be more concerning.
  • Companies with strong earnings/balance sheets can better absorb higher financing costs than leveraged borrowers.
  • View does not depend on a weaker U.S. dollar, though dollar weakness/stronger EM inflows would be additional support.

Key Monitoring

  • Flash PMIs (U.S., UK, Europe, Japan) for signs business activity holds up as borrowing costs rise.
  • Final University of Michigan consumer sentiment/inflation expectations — a further drop plus higher inflation expectations would tighten the Fed's trade-off.
  • Term premium trend — whether the yield rise stays "growth-driven" rather than "credibility-driven."
