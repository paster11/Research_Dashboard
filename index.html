BlackRock Investment Institute

31 weeklies, Jan 5 – Aug 10, 2026 · Latest: "Two market signals, one story" BII on summer pause — next edition Aug 31

Key Insights

  • Strong corporate earnings and rising government bond yields are read as the same signal, not contradictory ones: a structurally higher cost of capital that calls for a different portfolio approach.
  • BII's capital market assumptions put U.S. corporate earnings growth at 11.6%/yr over the next five years — a pace seen in only ~15% of historical five-year periods, conditional on AI adoption boosting productivity and margins.
  • UST yields fell this week as weak July payrolls eased pressure for an immediate Fed hike — a reversal from recent weeks' rising-yield narrative — but the 2s/30s gap widened ~20bps since the last Fed meeting, a steeper curve still signaling long-term inflation risk.
  • S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted their biggest weekly gains in three months on hopes of a Middle East peace deal plus solid earnings.

Key Takeaways (positioning)

  • Tactical asset-class table unchanged vs. the Aug 3 edition (verified line-by-line): OW U.S. equities, UW long USTs & JGBs, OW euro short/medium govt, OW agency MBS, OW EM local-currency debt.
  • Preference for equities, durable income and limited duration risk reaffirmed as the portfolio expression of the "structurally higher cost of capital" thesis on a 5-year+ horizon.
  • UW global IG credit maintained — tight spreads offer little compensation for duration risk; favor selected private credit (direct lending) for resilient cash flows.
  • Prefer growth exposure via equities and private infrastructure equity over high yield credit; reiterates this quarter's strategic HY underweight.

Key Outlook

  • July CPI (Aug 12) will show whether softer hiring/wages are feeding through to prices; BII expects some inflation rebound from June's softer reading — services inflation is the key watch item.
  • Week ahead: Aug 10 China total social financing; Aug 12 US CPI; Aug 13 US PPI, UK GDP; Aug 14 UK trade/flash employment/flash GDP.
  • Softer jobs report gives the Fed more near-term flexibility but does not materially alter BII's longer-term inflation outlook.

Key Monitoring

  • Whether July services inflation confirms or reverses June's softness.
  • Curve dynamics (2s/30s steepening) as a long-term inflation-risk signal even as front-end yields ease.
  • Middle East peace-deal developments and read-through to risk sentiment (drove this week's equity rally).
  • AI productivity/margin delivery — the conditioning assumption behind BII's above-consensus 5-year earnings growth call.

Invesco Solutions — Tactical Asset Allocation

April & July 2026 editions · Latest: July 1, 2026 no new edition as of Aug 5

Key Insights

  • Macro framework in a SLOWDOWN regime: growth above trend but decelerating; risk appetite modestly decelerating after strong AI-fueled returns.
  • Regime driver has shifted: from inflation uncertainty (April, energy shock) to policy constraint — falling inflation expectations with rising REAL rates = tighter financial conditions.
  • Q1-26 fundamentals strong: 85% of S&P 500 beat EPS; IT earnings +54%; positive guidance above 5- and 10-yr norms.
  • Hormuz reopened (~18 tankers/day vs. pre-conflict norm) but damaged energy infrastructure keeps supply risk alive.

Key Takeaways (positioning)

  • Moderate overweight equities vs. fixed income — but defensively expressed: OW quality & low volatility factors; favor IT, health care, staples over cyclicals.
  • Regional: incrementally reduced U.S. preference vs. DM ex-US, and DM vs. EM.
  • Fixed income: underweight credit risk; overweight duration as a growth hedge; now favor nominal Treasuries over inflation-linked.
  • USD flipped to underweight; favor EUR, JPY, CAD, NOK, SGD and high-yielding EM FX (INR, IDR, TWD, THB).

Key Outlook

  • Slowdown is a risk-management posture, not a cycle-ending signal; deceleration continues into H2.
  • Higher real yields are a headwind for growth assets and raise the odds the slowdown persists.
  • Rate convergence globally erodes USD carry appeal — supports non-US assets.

Key Monitoring

  • Leading economic indicators & global risk appetite (regime-change triggers).
  • Hormuz tanker traffic and energy-infrastructure rebuild timeline.
  • Q2 earnings season follow-through on AI super-cycle momentum.
  • Real-rate trajectory / financial-conditions tightening.

Key Outlook

Fidelity International — GAA Insights

Monthly, Multi Asset team · Latest: August 2026 (views July 2026) · Horizon 12–18 months

Key Insights

  • Global economy remains resilient: strong corporate earnings, fiscal expansion across major economies and continued AI-led investment; the Iran conflict has entered a "more complex phase" but markets are refocusing on fundamentals over near-term disruption.
  • AI investment cycle broadening beyond mega-cap tech into industrials, infrastructure, advanced manufacturing and selected financials.
  • Regional divergence increasing: inflation, policy and growth paths are differentiating more across regions, reinforcing the case for active management and selectivity.
  • Policy expectations shifting toward "higher-for-longer" as resilient U.S. growth and persistent inflation raise the odds the Fed stays restrictive.

Key Takeaways (positioning)

  • Remain overweight equities; still favour Japan (conviction OW: wage-led reflation, reforms) and EM (Korea, Taiwan, China tech); Europe remains least-preferred region.
  • Credit stays cautious overall: spreads (especially IG) offer little compensation despite resilient fundamentals; high yield remains an income-only opportunity.
  • Government bonds neutral/differentiated by region; UK Gilts remain the preferred developed-market duration exposure.
  • NEW this month: moved underweight the euro (weaker earnings momentum, softer growth, widening U.S./Europe divergence) — remain OW USD and UW sterling; gold retained as diversifier though near-term tailwinds have eased.

Key Outlook

  • AI investment cycle remains the dominant growth driver; broadening leadership supports opportunities beyond the largest tech names.
  • Regional policy divergence widening — differing inflation trends and central-bank paths create a wider range of opportunities and risks across asset classes.
  • Watching whether Middle East developments cause renewed disruption or allow markets to stay focused on fundamentals.

Key Monitoring

  • Geopolitics and energy markets — whether the Middle East situation disrupts markets or fades into the background.
  • Breadth of AI-led earnings growth beyond the largest technology companies.
  • Regional policy divergence and how differing central-bank paths shape asset-class opportunities.
